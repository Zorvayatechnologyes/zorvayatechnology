📚 Strategy Guide — What We Use & How It Works
Tap any strategy below to read exactly what it does and when to trust it. 10 strategies work together on every scan — all running silently in the background.
⚡Agent 1 — Momentum StrategyRSI · MACD · MA▶
What it is

The classic momentum strategy: it measures how fast and how hard price is moving, and whether that move is running out of steam. It uses three indicators together — RSI (14), MACD (12, 26, 9) and moving averages (SMA 20 / EMA 50 / EMA 200).

How it works
BUY whenRSI is recovering from below 30–40 (oversold bounce), MACD line crosses above its signal line (momentum turning up), and price is above the 200 EMA (big trend supports the long).
SELL whenRSI rolls over from above 60–70, MACD crosses down, and price sits below the 200 EMA.

Each condition votes bullish or bearish — the agent counts the votes and outputs a BUY, SELL or WAIT with a confidence percentage. Stops and targets are ATR-based (1× ATR stop, 2× ATR target).

Best used when

Markets are trending steadily. In flat, choppy ranges momentum signals arrive late — that is what the other agents are for.

🎯Agent 2 — Liquidity Sweep StrategySweep · FVG · Pivots▶
What it is

A professional price-action strategy (from your own strategy document). It hunts the moment big players grab liquidity — stop-loss clusters sitting just beyond swing highs and lows — and then enter when price displaces back with intent.

How it works — the 3-step sequence
1 · SweepA candle's wick spikes through a recent pivot high or low (where stops are parked) but the candle closes back inside — the trap has been sprung.
2 · DisplacementThe next candles move strongly in the opposite direction, leaving a Fair Value Gap (FVG) — a 3-candle imbalance where candle 1's high never touches candle 3's low.
3 · EntryBuy/sell limit placed inside the FVG (institutional entry zone). Stop beyond the sweep wick, first target at the recent opposing liquidity pool. The 200 EMA acts as the direction filter, and the London/NY session timing weights the setup.
Best used when

Gold is ranging between clear highs and lows with lots of stop-hunts — typical during London and New York sessions, especially at their overlap.

📊Agent 3 — Bollinger Bands Volatility StrategyMean Reversion▶
What it is

A volatility strategy built on Bollinger Bands (20, 2.0) — a 20-period average with bands drawn 2 standard deviations above and below it. Statistically, price stays inside the bands ~95% of the time, so band touches are meaningful.

How it works
BUY whenprice touches the lower band while RSI is under 40 — oversold snap-back toward the middle band. Stop below the band − 1.5 ATR, target 75% of the way to the opposite side.
SELL whenprice touches the upper band with RSI above 60 — fade the stretch.
Squeezewhen the bands pinch to their narrowest in ~50 bars, energy is coiling — the agent flags WAIT — SQUEEZE so you watch for the breakout bar, then trades its direction.
Band walkif price keeps riding a band without falling back inside, that is a strong trend, not a reversal — the agent switches to continuation mode.
Best used when

Gold is ranging between support and resistance. Mean reversion is dangerous in runaway trends — the band-walk rule exists to catch that.

📈Agent 4 — Supertrend Trend-Following StrategyATR Trailing▶
What it is

The most popular trend-following system on TradingView, tuned here as Supertrend (10, 3.0). It draws a single trailing line under price in an uptrend and above price in a downtrend — the line is the stop.

How it works
The lineis placed at the midpoint of each candle ± 3 × ATR. In an uptrend it only ratchets UP (never drops), hugging price like a trailing stop-loss.
BUY whenprice closes above the line and the line flips to support below price — confirmed by the fast EMA 9 crossing above EMA 21. A flip within the last 3 bars gets extra confidence (trend ignition).
SELL whenprice closes below the line and it flips to resistance above — with EMA 9 below EMA 21. Stop sits just beyond the Supertrend line, target 2 × ATR.
Best used when

Gold is trending hard (news days, rate decisions, dollar moves). In choppy ranges it flips too often — the EMA cross filter exists to cut false flips.

🔄RSI Divergence StrategyHidden Weakness▶
What it is

A weakness detector that compares swing extremes in price against the same swings in RSI. When the two disagree, the current move is running on fumes.

How it works
Bullishprice prints a lower low but RSI prints a higher low — sellers can no longer push price down with the same force. Reversal up is likely.
Bearishprice prints a higher high but RSI prints a lower high — buyers are exhausted even though price is rising. Reversal down is likely.

Fresh divergences (the second swing within the last 30 bars) score higher than old ones. It votes with the other strategies but never sets entry/exit levels — the stop always comes from the structure-anchored stop engine.

Best used when

After extended moves — divergence appears at exhaustion points, exactly where pure trend strategies are most confident and most wrong.

🏛️Market Structure StrategyHH/HL · LH/LL▶
What it is

The purest price-action read there is: it classifies the last four confirmed swing pivots into a structure.

How it works
Uptrendhigher highs + higher lows → BUY bias. Buy the pullbacks, don't fade the trend.
Downtrendlower highs + lower lows → SELL bias. Sell the rallies, don't catch the knife.
Contractinghigher lows + lower highs — a coil is forming; wait for the break.
Expandinglower lows + higher highs — volatility expansion, no clean edge yet.

It votes with the other strategies and never sets levels of its own.

Best used when

Every timeframe — on 15m it catches intraday swing shifts, on 1D it defines the regime every other strategy should respect.

🛡️Agent 5 — Stop-Loss GuardianStructure Stop▶
What it is

The agent that fixes the stop-loss problem. Older engines (including this app before Agent 5) used a blind "entry ± 1.5 × ATR" stop — it ignored where price actually reverses, so it was either too tight (wicked out for nothing) or too wide (huge risk). The Guardian anchors the stop to market structure instead.

How it computes the official stop
1 · Anchorfind the 20-bar swing low (for longs) or 20-bar swing high (for shorts) — the level where the trade idea is actually wrong.
2 · Bufferplace the stop 0.5 × ATR beyond the swing, so ordinary stop-hunt wicks don't reach it.
3 · Sanity clampsif the swing is more than 3 × ATR away (deep pullback) risk is capped at 3 ATR; if price is sitting right on the swing, a minimum breathing-room buffer is kept.
4 · Exitsit also publishes the invalidation level (a candle CLOSE beyond the swing kills the trade — exit, don't argue) and the breakeven trigger (once price moves 1R your way, move the stop to entry and trade risk-free).
Where you see it

The main signal card's Stop Loss value and its sub-label (“swing low 2,591.40 ± 0.5 ATR · risk $8.20”) come from this agent, the SL line on the analysis chart is drawn from it, and the Position Size chip sizes the trade from it. Agent 5 does not vote on direction — Agents 1–4 decide whether to trade; Agent 5 decides where the trade is wrong.

🏁Consensus Engine — How the Agents VoteVoting Rules▶
What it is

The judge. Six voting strategies (momentum, structure/sweep, volatility, trend, divergence, market structure) scan the same candles in parallel in a few milliseconds, then their verdicts are counted as votes — long, short or wait. One agreed direction is worth far more than six separate opinions. The engine runs silently in the background: the app shows only the final verdict, the strategic entry, the structure-anchored stop-loss and the targets. The stop engine (Stop-Loss Guardian) anchors the official stop-loss, risk per ounce, invalidation level and breakeven trigger to the winning side.

The voting rules
Unopposedno strategy opposes → BUY / SELL — full planned position size.
Wide agreementwith one STRONG verdict → STRONG BUY / STRONG SELL.
Majorityclear majority but some strategies fight → CAUTIOUS — half size or wait.
Small minorityonly one or two strategies see an edge → CAUTIOUS — half size, or wait for friends.
Splitstrategies split evenly on direction → CONFLICT — STAND ASIDE. No trade. This rule saves you from the worst trades.

Trade levels come from the best-aligned agent, preferring the Structure agent's precise levels (FVG entry) when it agrees with the vote — and the official stop-loss always comes from Agent 5's structure-anchored calculation, with R:R recomputed against it.

🎯Trade Plan — Where to Buy / Where to SellLimit Zones▶
What it is

Instead of chasing price, the Trade Plan computes resting limit-order zones on both sides — the green BUY zone below price and the red SELL zone above — and draws them on the chart.

How each zone is picked (in priority order)
1sta fresh unfilled FVG just below (for buys) or above (for sells) price — the institutional entry price.
2ndthe nearest confirmed swing pivot — support (swing low) for buying, resistance (swing high) for selling.
3rdthe lower/upper Bollinger Band — statistically stretched price.
Fallbackan ATR pullback zone (0.6–1.2 × ATR away from price).

Stop-loss goes 1 × ATR beyond the zone; TP1 takes partial profit at half your R:R setting and TP2 is the full target (change 1:3 → 1:2 in the risk strip and both move instantly). The card marked ★ is the side the agents favour, and if price crashes a zone the plan self-heals to the next level.

Best used when

Every session — place your orders at the zone and let price come to you. Status shows IN ZONE (act now), WAIT FOR PULLBACK (don't chase) or INVALIDATED.

💼Risk Management — The 1% Rule & Position SizingSurvival▶
What it is

The part that decides whether you survive long enough to be profitable. All values in the risk strip are editable and saved on your device.

How it works
1% rulerisk a maximum of 1% of your account per trade (editable) — so a losing streak is a scratch, not a disaster.
R : Rminimum 1:3 reward per unit risked (editable) — you can be wrong more often than right and still profit.
Limits2% daily loss limit, 3 consecutive losses, 10 trades per session — your circuit breakers.
Position sizecomputed live: risk% of $10,000 ÷ (entry − stop distance) → exact lots or ounces. A $30 stop distance at 1% risk = 0.03 lots. Change the risk % and the size recalculates instantly.
The math

Lots = (Account × Risk%) ÷ (Stop distance × 100). One standard XAU/USD lot = 100 ounces, so a $5,000 risk budget over a $50 stop = 1.00 lot.
